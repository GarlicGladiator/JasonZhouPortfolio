Field Notes

What I've learned out there

Every mechanic is a promise. The moment a player learns a rule, they start testing where it breaks — and my job is to make sure the edges hold, so breaking them feels like mastery instead of a glitch.

I chart a system on paper before I prototype it, and prototype before I pitch it. Numbers tell the truth a rulebook hides. I've led design on shipped titles and gone it alone in a weekend jam, and both trips taught me the same thing: cut the mechanic before you cut the clarity.

  • ✎

    Legible feedback loops

    If a player can't explain why they won or lost, the system failed before the level did.

  • ✎

    Friction is a tool

    Difficulty isn't the enemy of fun — unexplained difficulty is.

  • ✎

    Playtest early, playtest ugly

    A grey-box level tells the truth a beautiful mockup never will.

  • ✎

    Chart the numbers first

    Balance the economy on paper — story can justify a system, it can't fix one.
